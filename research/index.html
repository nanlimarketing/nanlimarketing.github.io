Working Papers

  1. Price Cuts Without Savings: Moral Hazards in Volume-Based Centralized Procurement
    Nan Li, Xu Li, Yuanchen Li, and 2 more authors
    2026
    Abs HTML

    Centralized procurement is designed to lower input prices by pooling demand across downstream providers. Whether these price reductions translate into lower spending for final users, however, remains unclear. Procurement may cover only one component of a product bundle, whereas downstream providers retain discretion over the rest. In this paper, we study this question in the context of China’s National Volume-Based Procurement (NVBP) reform, a national government-run group purchasing program for pharmaceuticals. Using a difference-in-differences design, we analyze a unique patient-visit-level panel of outpatient visits from multiple hospitals in a Chinese province. We find that NVBP reduces patients’ spending on disease-related core drugs by 16.4%. However, much of the resulting savings is offset, leaving total medical expenditure without a statistically or economically meaningful decline. We identify two channels driving this offset. First, providers expand the quantity and expenditure of non-procured auxiliary prescriptions. This pattern is consistent with providers shifting prescriptions toward components of the treatment bundle that remain outside the procurement contract. Second, patients visit hospitals more frequently and experience more active adjustment of their core-drug regimens, with more core-drug items changed between consecutive visits and more items new to their prescription histories. These regimen changes indicate that procurement affected the broader process of medication management. We further show that savings concentrate in non-tertiary hospitals located in the provincial capital. Taken together, our findings suggest that the procurement price is only an intermediate measure of performance when the buyer contracts on one component of the product bundle and is not the final user. The final savings also depend on how downstream providers adjust decisions that remain outside the procurement contract.

  2. Non-Usage in Digital Marketplaces: Evidence from Eight Years of Steam Usage
    Avery Haviv and Nan Li
    2026
    Abs HTML

    In this descriptive paper, we study non-usage, where consumers purchase products that they never use. We assemble a panel dataset from Steam, the world’s largest PC gaming platform, linking purchases to subsequent usage over an eight-year window for tens of thousands of users. Non-usage is widespread: 28.8 percent of purchased games are never played, representing 19.4 percent of revenue from game sales and roughly $512 million in 2017 spending. Discounted purchases and purchases during sales events are less likely to go unused. Non-usage rises sharply when games are bought several at a time, and falls with a game’s quality. After buying a game that goes unused, consumers do not reduce their subsequent purchasing, spending, or playtime. We discuss which explanations for non-usage are consistent with these patterns, and draw implications for consumers, platforms, and regulators.

  3. The Spillover Effects of Copycat Apps and App Platform Governance
    Jingcun Cao, Avery Haviv, and Nan Li
    Feb 2025
    Abs DOI HTML

    From taking inspiration to outright copying, new apps are frequently developed using the ideas of successful competitors. Mobile app platforms (markets), such as the Apple App Store and the Google Play Store, have policies intended to screen these "copycat” apps. We study the effect these "copycat” apps have on incumbents by leveraging data from multiple Android app markets in China. We identify which apps copied others using a supervised machine-learning approach. Our identification strategy leverages the fact that we observe the same app competing against different competitors on different platforms. We find that on average copycats do not reduce the demand for incumbent apps. However, moderators of this effect show that the largest and most highly rated apps are harmed by the presence of copycats. Furthermore, copycats cause developers of incumbent apps to behave differently. Higher-quality incumbents change their descriptions to differentiate themselves from low-quality copycats. Our results show that the filtering of copycat apps does not confer significant benefits to app developers, which may inform recent discussions on the value that app platforms provide.

  1. Platform Information Design and Competitive Price Targeting
    Ruiqi Wu, Yufeng Huang, and Nan Li
    Marketing Science, 2026
    Accepted at Marketing Science
    Abs HTML

    We study platform information design, in which a platform provides information about individual consumer preferences, and sellers use the information to set targeted prices. Because targeting might intensify competition within narrow markets, what information should be provided to improve sellers’ profits remains an empirical question. Using unique individual-level purchase and usage data on Steam, we estimate a two-stage consumer choice model to recover heterogeneous preferences for video games. Then, we simulate and compare across a comprehensive set of counterfactual targeted price equilibria, where sellers are given no, full, or partial information about individual consumer types through (coarsened) segmentation. Our results show that the platform can maximize sellers’ total profits without providing full information, achieving a 71.9 million annual profit gain. Moreover, a coarsened, two-segment design can already achieve a 55.7 million profit gain by revealing the aspect of heterogeneity that leads to market expansion while concealing the aspects that lead to intensified competition.

  2. Opposing Influences of YouTube Influencers: Purchase and Usage Effects in the Video Game Industry
    Nan Li, Avery Haviv, and Mitchell J. Lovett
    Markting Science, Jan 2025
    Abs DOI HTML

    Influencers promote firms’ products by posting content such as videos on social media platforms. For entertainment products, these posts could substitute or complement demand for the original entertainment product. We study video games, the largest entertainment product category comprising 1/3 of YouTube traffic, using a large daily panel data set on thousands of video games. Leveraging a supply shock on YouTube called the "Adpocalypse", we measure the impact of influencer videos on purchase and usage of games. We provide plausibly causal evidence that on average influencer video posts substitute to video games for purchases but complement for usage. We also find that influencer effects differ across firms. Managers can use these results to align the influencer effects they face with their revenue models, such as using in-game purchases or a subscription model when facing complements on usage.

  3. Intertemporal Demand Spillover Effects on Video Game Platforms
    Avery Haviv, Yufeng Huang, and Nan Li
    Management Science, Mar 2020
    Finalist, 2023 Management Science marketing dept. best paper awards
    Abs DOI HTML

    Many platform strategies focus on indirect network effects between sellers through platform expansion. In this paper, we show sellers on the console video game platform generate a positive intertemporal spillover effect and expand the demand for other sellers, holding the set of platform adopters fixed. We propose a novel identification strategy that leverages exogenous variation in the release timing of games exclusively available on a console platform, and examine how this variation affects the sales of games available on both platforms. We find a sizable intertemporal demand spillover effect between games: A 1% increase in total copies sold on a platform leads to a 0.153% increase in the sales of other games in the next month (i.e., an elasticity of 0.153). Additional analysis suggests this demand spillover effect is reminiscent of habit formation on the consumer side, in that past purchases keep end users active on the platform. Our finding provides a potential explanation for recent platform sales events and subscription services that provide free games to consumers every month.
